## The brief

> Twelve small quizzes, not one large one. Show up, answer what that lecture
> just covered, and let the best seven speak for the other five.

There is no artefact to prepare here and nothing to hand in. A quiz opens on
the first slide of every lecture, delivered by a QR code, asks about that
lecture's content and nothing further back or forward, and closes before the
next one starts.

## Two tracks, one question

The room and the stream see the same question, on two different clocks.

**In the room.** Two minutes, and you may talk it over with whoever is next to
you. Turning up without having done the reading is entirely recoverable here —
that is what the two minutes and the room are for.

**Watching online.** About thirty seconds — long enough to answer if you
already know it, not long enough to open a second tab and go looking. Doing
well here means the week's page was read before the lecture started, not
during it.

Why the two clocks differ, and what that says about the course's own subject,
is on the [arc page](/arc/), next to the timeline this component sits inside
of.

## What counts

Twelve quizzes run across the semester. The **best seven of those twelve**
scores are the ones that count, worth 20% of the course. The other five are
free to lose — to a missed lecture, a slow morning, or a question that was
simply hard to answer in thirty seconds — with no explanation owed and nothing
lost beyond that quiz's own mark.

## How this is marked

Automatically, at the moment each quiz closes: correct or not, with no partial
credit and no re-mark. The judgement this component asks for is not in any one
answer — it is in showing up, and having read enough of the week to be worth
two minutes of somebody's attention.

## What you submit

Nothing prepared in advance. Be in the room or watching live, answer inside
the window you are given, and let the best seven of twelve stand for the rest.
