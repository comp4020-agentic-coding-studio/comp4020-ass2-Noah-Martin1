I worked emergency medicine for eleven years, which means I have personally
applied an ordering rule to roughly a hundred thousand people, under time
pressure, with incomplete information, knowing that some of those decisions were
wrong.

Triage is the case this course keeps coming back to, and I am here partly to
complicate it. Week 7 will teach you that ordering by need beats ordering by
arrival, which is true. What that framing hides is how much work the word "need"
is doing. Triage categories are a model. They compress a person into one of five
buckets in ninety seconds, they systematically under-read pain in patients who
do not present it the way the category description expects, and the people most
often mis-sorted are the ones with the least practice at being believed.

So when Adaeze says a queue's ordering rule is doing distributive work, I would
add: the rule is only as good as the measurement it runs on, and the measurement
is made by a tired human at 3am. That disagreement is real, it is not staged for
teaching, and you will see us have it in week 7.
