I ran turnarounds. That means I was responsible for the interval between an
aircraft arriving at a gate and the same aircraft leaving it, and for most of
that interval the binding constraint was people walking down an aisle at
different speeds while carrying bags of different sizes.

The literature on boarding is unusually good. There are strategies — you will
meet several in week 10 — that are demonstrably twenty to thirty per cent faster
than what most airlines do. Almost none of them are used. The reasons are
interesting and almost entirely non-technical: they interfere with selling
priority boarding, they separate families, they are hard to explain at a gate,
and they fail badly when a handful of passengers ignore them. I think that gap
between the best known algorithm and the deployed one is the most useful thing
an operations person can show a student, and it is why I teach here.

I take the labs, which means I am the person who will read your raw counts and
ask why you stopped the stopwatch when you did. I am pedantic about measurement
and unapologetic about it: nearly every wrong conclusion in this field starts
with a number somebody collected carelessly and then trusted.
