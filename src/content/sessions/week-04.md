## Before the lab

Bring your Queue Census data — yours, or a classmate's if your own queue turned
out to be unmeasurable. We are going to put a price on it.

## In the lab

**Costing, three ways.** Take your queue's total person-hours per day and cost
it under three different valuations:

1. every hour valued identically, at the median local wage;
2. every hour valued at what that specific population plausibly earns;
3. every hour valued at zero, which is the valuation the organisation running
   the queue is currently, implicitly, using.

Write all three numbers on the board next to your queue's name.

**The argument.** Then we have the argument the numbers force, and it is the
first properly contested session of the semester. Valuation (2) is the
economically orthodox one, and it will tell you to serve the wealthy first.
Valuation (1) is the one most people's instincts prefer, and it is not a
measurement — it is a moral commitment wearing a spreadsheet. Valuation (3) is
what almost every real system does, and it is not neutral either; it is the one
that produces the 300,000 hours in the lecture.

You will be asked to state which you would put in a public report, and why. You
may change your mind later in the semester — several people do, in both
directions, usually around week 8 — but you have to commit today.

## Afterwards

Keep the three numbers. When you write the final design brief you will have to
state what your queue optimises for, and this is the lab where most people first
notice that they had been assuming an answer.

Act I ends here. Next week the discipline knob starts turning.
