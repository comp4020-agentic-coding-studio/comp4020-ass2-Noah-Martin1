## Before the lab

Read the week 10 notes. Bring a bag — a real one, with something in it, that has
to go in an overhead locker.

## In the lab

We tape out a single-aisle cabin in the studio: six rows, three seats a side,
chairs for the seats and a shelf rack for the lockers. You are the passengers.
Tomáš runs the gate.

**Four runs.** Back-to-front, random, outside-in, Steffen. Same twenty-four
people, same bags, same seat assignments every time — only the call order
changes. One person on the stopwatch, two observing where the aisle blocks.

Expect back-to-front to be slowest and to feel the most organised while it
happens. That combination is the lesson.

**Then the model.** Run the same four strategies in the simulator with your
measured stow times. Compare.

**The disagreement is the deliverable.** Your physical times will not match the
simulation, and the gap is where the actual learning is. Candidates the room
usually finds: people in the room are unusually compliant and unusually
bag-light; nobody in the room has a child, a walking frame, or a language
barrier; nobody is arguing about a locker; and everybody has already read the
lecture, which makes them the least representative passengers in aviation.

Steffen degrades hardest under non-compliance. If there is time, we run it once
more with four people deliberately ignoring their call and watch the advantage
evaporate — which is a large part of why the airline does not use it.

## Afterwards

Write down the one effect you saw in the room that your model does not contain.
Bring it to the week 12 lab; it is exactly the kind of thing the final brief has
to be honest about.
