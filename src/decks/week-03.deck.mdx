## Same area, two cuts

**Count across** — how many are here right now? Average it. That is **L**.

**Follow one through** — how long did this one stay? Average it. That is **W**.

Both are measuring **person-minutes**.

---

## The proof, in four lines

1. Window of length *T*. *N* people pass through.
2. Total time inside = *A*. So **W = A/N**.
3. Same *A*, integrated over the window: **L = A/T**.
4. And **λ = N/T**.

L/λ = (A/T)÷(N/T) = A/N = **W**

```notes
Do this on the board, not on the slide. The T cancelling is the moment. It is a
fact about rectangles, not about queues.
```

## What it is for

**Estimate** a wait from outside.

**Size** something you have not built.

**Catch a lie** — 500 people, 20 a minute, and they claim 10 minutes.

**Sanity-check a promise** — halve W with λ fixed, and L must halve. Which half
of these people is leaving?

## It is not really about queues

- Factory: **WIP = throughput × cycle time**
- Kanban: **lead time = WIP ÷ throughput**
- Warehouse: inventory turns
- Support desk: 300 open ÷ 60 a day = **5 days**
- Hospital: beds = admissions × length of stay

```notes
The support desk one lands hardest. A team promising 48-hour resolution while
carrying 300 open tickets is in arithmetic contradiction with itself, and no
amount of effort fixes it.
```

---

## Three ways to get it wrong

**It was filling or emptying.** Steady state, or nothing.

**Different windows.** L at 12:15 and λ across three hours are two systems.

**People left without being served.** They were in L. Were they in λ?

```notes
The third is week 11's entire population, and the most informative people in
any system are the ones who are not in its data.
```
