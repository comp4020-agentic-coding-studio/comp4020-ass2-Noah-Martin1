## So the rational move is always the same

- run at 97% utilisation
- close a window
- add a check that costs you 30 seconds and the queue 40 minutes

Each is correct, given what the manager is scored on.

```notes
This is the slide where somebody says "bad management". Resist it. Every one of
these decisions is right on the information available. The failure is upstream,
in what gets measured.
```

## Your hour is already priced

Transport appraisal publishes **values of travel time savings** — per person,
per journey purpose — and builds motorways on them.

Business travel near the employer's cost. Commuting and leisure lower, from
what people actually pay for speed.

**Published. Argued about. On the page.**

```notes
The contrast is the lecture. This is not an unsolved problem. It is a solved
problem the passport office has no reason to apply, because the hours are not
its hours.
```

## At what rate, though?

**What they'd pay to avoid it** → a barrister's hour is worth forty pensioners'.

**All hours equal** → a moral commitment wearing a spreadsheet.

**Zero** → what the office is doing right now.

## And it is not only hours

- unoccupied feels longer than occupied
- before the process feels longer than during it
- uncertain feels longer than known
- unexplained feels longer than explained
- **unfair feels longest of all**

```notes
Maister, mid-1980s. The last one is the one every service person in the room
already knows: one person served out of turn poisons the whole line, and
everybody can tell you exactly what it cost them, which is never the real
figure.
```

## The uncomfortable extension

An **ordeal mechanism**: make the benefit costly in time, so only the needy
bother.

Assumes the poor are time-rich.

```notes
Ask the room who is the most time-poor person in a benefits office. Somebody
will get to the parent on two hourly shifts with no leave. The screen selects
on flexibility of schedule, which correlates with income in the direction
nobody intended.
```
