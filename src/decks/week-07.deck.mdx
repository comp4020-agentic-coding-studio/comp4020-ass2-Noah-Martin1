## Every other priority rule gets resisted.

## Why does this one get consent?

```notes
Because the outcomes are not commensurable. The wrist gives up thirty minutes
of discomfort; the chest pain keeps their life. Nobody is tempted to compare
them.

General rule, slightly cynical: resistance depends less on the logic of the
rule than on how unlike each other the consequences are.
```

## Where it came from

*trier* — to sort

**Larrey**, Napoleon's surgeon: treat by severity of wound, disregard rank.

```notes
The rule triage replaced was not first-come-first-served. It was sort by social
position. The innovation was "stop using something else", and it had to be
imposed against the interests of the people it demoted.
```

---

## And the part nobody discusses

Mass-casualty tags: immediate · delayed · minor · **expectant**

Sickest-first and most-lives-saved are **different rules**.

In a disaster they disagree.

```notes
In a normally resourced department they mostly agree, which is why everybody
can support triage without ever being asked which of the two they support.
Week 12 is where that finally has to be answered.
```

## And a fourth, belonging to the institution

The classifier is an **input to the performance report**.

A borderline case carded down is a borderline case that cannot breach.

```notes
Nobody has to be dishonest for this to bend. There is always a defensible
clinical story for either choice.
```

## 15% wrong. How much does it degrade?

Category 1 mean: **6 → 9 minutes**

Category 3 mean: **98 → 91 minutes** *(better!)*

Category 1 waiting over 30 min: **0.7% → 3.0%**

```notes
A monthly report with the first two numbers in it would not raise an eyebrow
anywhere. The third is four times as many people having heart attacks in the
waiting room.
```
