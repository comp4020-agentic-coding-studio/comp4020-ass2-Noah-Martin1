## Two blocking events

**The stow** — variable duration, and the variance is the cost

**The seat shuffle** — pure self-inflicted damage, caused entirely by the order
people were let on

---

## Four strategies

**Back to front** — what many airlines use

**Random** — scatters people along the aisle

**Outside-in (WILMA)** — kills the seat shuffle

**Steffen** — alternating rows, outside-in. Consecutive boarders two rows apart

## Calling rows 30 to 36

puts every bag-stowing passenger into the same five metres of aisle at the same
moment.

You have carefully **concentrated** the interference.

```notes
Which is exactly what week 2 told them not to do. Say it in those words.
```

## And it was tested with real people

Proposed 2008. Field experiment with a mock cabin, volunteers and luggage
broadly confirmed it.

Roughly half the time of back-to-front.

# So why does nobody use it?

---

## Three reasons, none of them technical

**It fights the revenue model.** Zone 1 is a product.

**It separates people travelling together.**

**It is hard to explain** at a gate, in a second language, over a poor PA.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

Rank your options by how they perform

when **half-followed**,

not by how they perform when obeyed.

```notes
Steffen is best under compliance and degrades fast. Random degrades not at all
because it has nothing to degrade from. Outside-in survives families, and is
what the airlines that improved anything actually adopted.
```

---

## The gate is a network

deplane → clean → **board**

fuel · cater · load, partly in parallel

```notes
The turnaround takes as long as the longest chain of things that must happen in
order. Everything off that chain has slack.
```

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## You speed up boarding by four minutes.

## How much shorter is the turnaround?

```notes
Between four minutes and nothing, and you cannot tell which without looking at
the rest of the gate. Improving a non-bottleneck is not a small win. It is zero.
```

---

## Bottlenecks move

**Check-in → security.** Same journey time, smaller room.

**Braess, 1968.** Adding a road can make every journey longer.

**Emergency departments.** The bottleneck is discharge, upstairs.

**Bufferbloat.** More buffer, more latency, no more throughput.

---

{/* _class: quote */}

Back-to-front does not create interference.

It arranges for the interference to happen

where the queue is standing.
