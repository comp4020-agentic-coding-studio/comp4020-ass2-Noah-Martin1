# People are not jobs

SLOP2710 · Week 11

```notes
Print jobs arrive, wait however long they are made to, and leave. Every model
in this course so far has assumed people do the same. They do not.
```

---

## Three things jobs never do

**Balk** — see the queue, don't join

**Renege** — join, then leave

**Game** — find the rule, optimise against it

---

{/* _class: quote */}

A queue with heavy balking

looks exactly like a queue with low demand.

```notes
Short lines, low utilisation, happy numbers. Only one of them has a problem,
and the only way to tell is to count from outside: footfall past the door
against transactions at the till. Almost nobody does this.
```

---

## And reneging is what stabilises an overloaded queue

Week 2's table has no row for ρ > 1. Real overloaded systems still reach an
equilibrium.

**The length of an overloaded queue measures its customers' patience, not its
capacity.**

## You install an accurate live wait estimate.

## What happens to its accuracy?

```notes
It destroys itself. Announce nine minutes, some people hang up who would have
stayed, the queue shortens, the wait falls — and the number you announced is
now wrong because you announced it.
```

## Counting the people who left

Erlang's formulas assume callers wait as long as it takes.

**Erlang A** adds one parameter — patience — and changes what every other number
means.

---

## Which gives you a trap

More agents → wait down, abandonment down

Better hold experience → abandonment down, **measured wait up**

```notes
Because more of the people who would have hung up are still there to be counted.
A service-level report carrying only one of these numbers is not interpretable.
```

---

{/* _class: quote */}

A system that makes waiting **feel** shorter

has removed its own pressure

to make waiting **be** shorter.

---

## What should the announcement say?

**Nothing** — maximises the most expensive feature of a wait

**A position** — honest, verifiable, uninterpretable

**A time** — most useful, and it eats itself

---

{/* _class: centered */}

## So announce conservatively.

## Credibility is the asset.

```notes
Quote the upper end and most people beat it. You lose a few more to balking and
you keep the announcement believable, which is what makes every future
announcement work.
```

## Gaming, properly

**Scalping is a queueing attack** — FCFS defeated by being better at coming
first

The resale price measures what the original rule left on the table

```notes
US banned circumvention of purchase controls in 2016. Enforcement is the
problem, as always, when the attack is indistinguishable from an enthusiastic
customer.
```

---

## Three responses

**Harden** — an arms race the defender loses on cost

**Randomise** — gives up arrival-order fairness, buys immunity to speed

**Price it** — week 8; capture the value instead of burning it
