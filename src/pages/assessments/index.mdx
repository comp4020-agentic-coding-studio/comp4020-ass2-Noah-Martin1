# Assessment

Four pieces, and each one can only be attempted with the tools the weeks before
it handed over. That is deliberate: the assessment follows the same arc as the
syllabus rather than sitting beside it.

You measure a real queue in week 3, having just learned how. You change who goes
first in week 6, having spent two weeks on what that costs. You simulate the
change in week 9. Then you spend the back half of semester on one design brief
worth half the course.

Three things are worth knowing before you read the briefs. **Extra capacity is
not an answer** — the week 6 task forbids it, because it is the response that
lets you avoid thinking about priority at all. **Naming who is worse off carries
real marks**, because the mathematics makes it very easy to forget that anybody
is. And **the final brief is marked hardest on the alternative you rejected**,
argued at its strongest rather than its most convenient.
