# Lectures

Twelve lectures, in three acts. Act I is the physics — why queues form, how to
measure one from the outside, and what waiting costs. Act II is five ordering
rules, one a week, each of which buys something and charges somebody. Act III is
what happens when the queue turns out to be connected to other queues and full
of people who have worked out what you are doing.

Weeks 2, 5, 8 and 10 carry a simulation you can drive yourself. They are not
illustrations of the argument; in two cases the simulation is what corrected it.

If you want to see how the weeks depend on each other before reading any of
them, [the shape of the semester](/arc/) lays out the chain.
