# Lectures

Twelve lectures, in three acts. Act I is the physics — why queues form, how to
measure one from the outside, and what waiting costs. Act II is five ordering
rules, one a week, each of which buys something and charges somebody. Act III is
what happens when the queue turns out to be connected to other queues and full
of people who have worked out what you are doing.

**Every week carries a simulation you can drive yourself**, set in a real place
— a café you rearrange, an airport line you measure two ways, an aircraft at a
gate, a dialysis unit where the rule decides who lives. They are not
illustrations of the argument. In several cases the simulation is what
corrected it, and each lecture says so where it happened.

Every week also has a **slide deck**, linked at the top of its page and carrying
the same live simulation, so the lecture in the room and the lecture on the site
are the same lecture rather than two accounts of it.

Allow twenty minutes a week: about fifteen to read, and the rest for the
controls, which is where most of the actual learning is.

If you want to see how the weeks depend on each other before reading any of
them, [the shape of the semester](/arc/) lays out the chain and shows where the
four pieces of assessment fall against it.
